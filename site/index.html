Presentations where the audience takes part

Write your talk in Markdown. People scan a QR code and vote, guess, write and ask from their phones, and the results land on your slides as they arrive. It all runs from your laptop.

Fifteen minutes on how language models write, with 24 simulated people in the room. Click the slide or use the arrow keys. On a slide with a question, the first click opens voting, the next one closes it.

A poll is a slide

No second tab, no separate account, no copy of your questions somewhere else. You write the interaction where it happens, in the same file as everything else.

---
title: Lunch and learn
---

::: poll {#lunch} Where should we go for lunch?
- Tacos
- Ramen
- [x] The place with the long queue

The queue is long for a reason.
:::

poll and multi
Live bars. Mark a right answer and it stays hidden until you reveal it.
estimate
Everyone guesses a number. Every guess becomes a dot, then the average, then the truth.
rounds
The same question over a list, one result per round.
sentence
The room writes a sentence one word at a time, like a language model picking the next token.
questions
Anonymous questions with upvotes. You approve them before they reach the screen.
component
Anything else, in a JavaScript file next to your talk.

Build what nobody has built yet

Add audience: to a component and every phone gets a text box, a number field or a list of options. Your code gets the answers. Quorum handles the rest: opening and closing, the QR code, rehearsal with simulated answers, the chat fallback, and keeping your screen and your panel in sync.

The examples include a word cloud and a next-word explorer that asks a language model running on your own laptop for its ten most likely words, so the room can watch it think.

How components work

<!-- talk.md -->
::: component word-cloud
audience: text
prompt: One word: how do you feel about AI?
:::

// components/word-cloud.js
export default {
  mount(el, ctx) {
    const paint = (c) => {
      el.textContent = c.answers.values.join(' ');
    };
    paint(ctx);
    return { update: paint };
  },
};

No accounts, no cloud

Your laptop is the server

One command starts it, opens a public link through a tunnel and puts the QR code on your slides. Votes stay in your talk folder until you delete them.

Rehearse alone

Rehearsal mode fills the room with simulated people, so you can time every vote before the day. The panel shows your notes, the next slides and your pace.

A plan B built in

If the link fails or the audience is on a call, press B. Phones are told to use the chat, and you click the options to count what you read there.
